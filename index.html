Fooled at First Sight? Eye-Tracking, Memory Recall, and Psychometric Modeling of Real vs. AI Image Judgments

Summary

Generative AI can now make images people can't reliably tell from real photos. We studied how people look at, judge, and remember AI versus real images, using eye tracking and two memory tests.

Research questions

  1. How do people visually process AI images compared with real photographs?
  2. How does memory for these images change over a short delay?
  3. How do image difficulty and individual ability relate to performance?

Method

42 participants viewed 18 pairs of images (one real photograph and one AI image matched by emotional category) and chose the AI image while their eye movements were recorded. They then completed two recognition memory tests, 10 minutes apart.

Key findings

  • AI images drew more attention but led to faster decisions.
  • After 10 minutes, people made more false memory errors, but their memory for images they had actually seen held steady.
  • Emotional images made AI easier to spot; nature scenes made it harder.

What people said

Participants relied on camera artifacts, unnatural human features, landscape realism, and scene simplicity to judge images. Most found the task difficult.

Next steps

This work is being prepared for publication. Additional analyses are ongoing.
